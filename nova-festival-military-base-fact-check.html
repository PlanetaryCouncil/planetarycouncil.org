Fact check · 7 October 2023 · Re'im, southern Israel

Was the Nova Festival Held on a Military Base?

The Nova (Supernova) music festival, the Hamas attack of 7 October 2023, the venue change, the extra day, and the Re'im army base nearby — what is established, what the official investigations say, what journalists reported, what is contested, and what is spin.

Published 30 September 2026 by Planetary Council — Ministry of Understanding, Educating and Sense-Making.

Verdict: False. The Nova festival was not held on a military base.

It took place in a forest clearing (the "Re'im parking lot") beside Road 232 near Kibbutz Re'im, about 4–5 kilometres from the Gaza border fence. The Re'im army base — headquarters of the Israeli army's Gaza Division — is in the same area, about 6.5 kilometres from the border.

Two related claims are true: Nova was a late add-on to an event already approved for the same site, and it ran into 7 October only because army officers, after first objecting, approved a one-day extension.

Why this page exists

Someone said the festival was held on a military base. Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence, so this page checks that claim and its context. No agenda beyond getting it right.

Key facts

  1. VERIFIEDLocation: forest clearing by Road 232, near Kibbutz Re'im, about 4–5 kilometres from Gaza. Not inside any military compound, and not a declared closed military zone.
  2. OFFICIALThe Re'im army base is about 6.5 kilometres from the border. Three festivalgoers reached it on foot and briefly entered a bomb shelter; they left and were later killed on Road 232. Four soldiers were killed defending the base.
  3. VERIFIED44 festivalgoers sheltered in Kibbutz Re'im; 43 survived.
  4. VERIFIEDDeath toll at the festival: 378 (344 civilians, 34 security personnel); 44 abducted to Gaza. This is the April 2025 army figure; older articles say about 364.

Timeline: permit, venue change and extension

Mainly from the Israeli army's own Nova investigation (April 2025) as reported by the Times of Israel.

  1. OFFICIAL6 September 2023 — the Nova production company asks police for a party called "Unity" at the Re'im parking lot, 5–6 October.
  2. OFFICIAL20 September — police authorise it without first consulting the army; the Northern Brigade receives the request the next day and approves Unity on 29 September.
  3. OFFICIAL2 October — request to extend the permit by 24 hours so Nova can reuse Unity's stages and equipment.
  4. REPORTEDVenue change — press accounts say Nova switched to Re'im about two days before, after another southern venue fell through. The reason is not publicly documented. The Re'im site had been booked for Unity weeks earlier; Nova was the late addition.
  5. OFFICIAL4 October — the Northern Brigade and Gaza Division object (fewer troops over the holiday weekend); after Southern Command discussions the extension is approved, late 6 October to afternoon 7 October.
  6. OFFICIAL6–7 October — about 3,500 attend. No army threat assessment; border troops not told the party exists; no soldiers assigned to it.
  7. VERIFIED06:29 — heavy rocket fire. The local police commander orders the party shut down; about 90 percent of attendees get away.
  8. OFFICIAL08:12–08:14 — attackers open fire on the police roadblock and hit it with a rocket-propelled grenade. 08:20 — one army tank reaches the area under heavy fire.
  9. OFFICIALAbout 09:15 — attackers enter the party area, where hundreds are hiding.
  10. OFFICIAL11:20–11:35 — first organised army force arrives (Ynet says 11:20; Times of Israel says infantry at 11:35, entering the compound at 11:50). Area declared cleared around 15:00 (one report says 03:00 the next morning).

The official narrative

Israeli army investigation led by Brigadier General Ido Mizrahi, April 2025; earlier police findings reported November 2023.

  1. OFFICIALNova was not a pre-planned Hamas target. The roughly 100 attackers who reached it were heading for Netivot, went the wrong way, then deliberately attacked the people they found. This is the army's reconstruction, not an independently tested finding.
  2. OFFICIALThe festival was legal and licensed; organisers followed the process.
  3. OFFICIALApproval followed a standing policy of not restricting civilian life near Gaza without a concrete threat.
  4. OFFICIAL"The army failed": no security coordination, no liaison officer, a false real-time picture, no contact with police.
  5. VERIFIEDGaza Division commander Avi Rosenfeld resigned in June 2024; Northern Brigade commander Haim Cohen in December 2024.

What journalists added

  1. REPORTEDGaza Division operations officer Lieutenant Colonel Sahar Fogel saw the event as a needless risk — from rockets and mortars, not a ground raid — but was told to approve it, partly over legal worries about refusing (Times of Israel, December 2023).
  2. REPORTEDThe army "bowed to pressure" from producers to approve the extra day; the Gaza Division commander reportedly said it would be the last festival he approved there (Haaretz, September 2025).
  3. REPORTEDThe only way to refuse was to declare the area a closed military zone; it was not declared one (secondary account citing Haaretz).
  4. REPORTEDPolice and army blame each other for the approval.
  5. REPORTEDVictims' families criticised the army probe for excluding the police and the Israel Security Agency (Shin Bet).

Contested: Israeli fire at Nova

  1. CONTESTEDHaaretz (18 November 2023) quoted one unnamed police source saying an army helicopter firing at attackers "apparently also hit some" festivalgoers. Israel Police denied its investigation assessed army fire. Not independently confirmed.
  2. REPORTEDHaaretz (July 2024) reported the "Hannibal" procedure — firing to stop abductions even at risk to captives — was invoked on 7 October at several sites including the Re'im base. Haaretz said it did not know whether or how many civilians were hit.
  3. REPORTEDHaaretz documented a separate case: a couple who escaped Nova were shot in the village of Alumim by a local security squad who mistook them for attackers; the man died.
  4. UNKNOWNHow many of the 378 deaths, beyond isolated cases, were caused by Israeli fire. No published official figure.

Spin — in both directions

  1. FALSE"The festival was held on a military base."
  2. OVERREACH"Israel admits it killed its own at Nova" — turns one unnamed, denied source about "some" people into an admission. Claims that Israel killed all of them have no evidence.
  3. FALSEA viral "leaked" helicopter video was unrelated to Nova (fact checks by France 24 and others, November 2023).
  4. CONFLATION"Hannibal was used at Re'im, so Israel killed the Nova crowd." The reported order concerned military sites; it is not evidence about who killed people at the festival field.
  5. UNSUPPORTED"Nova was a pre-planned Hamas target" — contradicted by Israel's own police and army findings.
  6. UNSUPPORTED"Partygoers were placed near the base on purpose / as a shield" — no evidence; records show a late venue addition and a reluctant, bureaucratic approval.

Most plausible explanation

Interpretation, built from the verified and official points above.

  1. Re'im is a crossroads: a junction on Road 232, a kibbutz, a forest clearing used for events, and the regional army headquarters, all close together.
  2. Hamas's plan hit army bases and road junctions. Fighters moving along Road 232 ran into thousands of people fleeing the rocket barrage.
  3. The site was approved because doctrine assumed rockets, not a mass ground raid, were the risk, and refusing required a formal closure the army was reluctant to impose.
  4. Holiday troop levels, no police coordination, and border units never told about the party turned proximity into catastrophe.
