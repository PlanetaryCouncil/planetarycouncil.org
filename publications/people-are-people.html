People are people.

Most people want to live in peace. Russians and Ukrainians, Israelis and Palestinians, Pakistanis and Indians, Americans too. Every religion and tradition already says it. Here are their own words, with a link to check every one.

This page was prompted by Israel's war on Gaza, and by the mindset behind it: from a politician who says the world must answer to Israel because “it says in the book”, to a teenager who tells Palestinians “leave or we'll kill you”. Both are quoted below. It is not neutral. It offers a perspective from outside Israel's official account, held to the same facts and the same rules for every side. On 7 October 2023 about 1,200 people were killed in Israel, most of them civilians, and 251 were taken hostage. That was a crime, and it is covered below. This page is mostly about what came after: two years of war, around 70,000 people killed in Gaza, and a ceasefire in which people are still being killed. Go straight to the part for Israel.

Real history

What happened, when, and why

The idea: most people prefer peace, but not at any price, and not always for everyone.

Wars usually come from the same few things: land and resources, money, power, fear and big ideas. Religion often gives the excuse. Sometimes it is a real reason too. Sometimes it holds people back.

How wars get sold

If most people prefer peace, how do wars start? Usually with a big shock or a big story that turns fear into anger. Sometimes the shock is real. Sometimes the story turns out to be false.

The Lusitania

7 May 1915 · First World War

What happened
A German submarine sank a British passenger liner off Ireland. About 1,200 people died, including 128 Americans.
The warning
The German embassy had sent a warning against travel, dated 22 April 1915, to about 50 American newspapers. It ran as the ship was about to leave New York on 1 May. The ship was also carrying ammunition and shell casings, listed on its cargo papers.
What followed
American opinion turned hard against Germany. The United States joined the war two years later, on 6 April 1917, after Germany restarted unlimited submarine attacks and offered Mexico an alliance in the Zimmermann Telegram.
Read more
Wikipedia

Pearl Harbor

7 December 1941 · Second World War

What happened
Japan launched a surprise attack on the American fleet in Hawaii. The United States entered the war the next day.
Code "Purple"
American code-breakers had cracked Japan's diplomatic code, called Purple, in 1940, and were reading its messages before the attack. Some writers argue President Roosevelt knew what was coming and let it happen.
What is known
Most historians reject that theory. One decoded spy message, sent in September 1941, told Japan’s agent in Hawaii to divide Pearl Harbor into five areas and report on the ships there. But no decoded message gave the date or the plan of the attack, and official inquiries blamed missed signals, poor coordination and underestimating Japan.
Please note
The claim that Pearl Harbor was allowed to happen is speculation. No credible evidence supports it.
Read more
National Archives · Wikipedia

9/11 and Iraq

2001 to 2003 · United States, United Kingdom and Iraq

What happened
Al-Qaeda attacked New York and Washington on 11 September 2001. In 2003 the United States and Britain invaded Iraq, saying it had weapons of mass destruction and links to al-Qaeda.
What was found
The stockpiles were not found. The official search team reported it had not found evidence that Saddam Husayn possessed WMD stocks in 2003. The 9/11 Commission found some contacts between Iraq and al-Qaeda, but no evidence that they developed into a collaborative operational relationship.
Read more
Iraq Survey Group · The 9/11 Commission finding · Reasons given for the war

Deception is part of war. Information is a weapon, and both sides use it. Before the Battle of Midway in 1942, the Americans had their base send a fake radio message saying its water supply had broken. When Japan repeated that "AF is short of water", the Americans knew Midway was the target. In 1943 British intelligence floated a dead body off Spain carrying fake secret papers, so that Germany would expect an invasion of Greece and Sardinia instead of Sicily. It worked. Read more: Battle of Midway · Operation Mincemeat

The pattern: people are people, and most people do not want to go to war. So a reason has to be found. Sometimes it is a real attack. Sometimes it is a story built to influence (manipulate) public opinion. Usually it is a shock, then fear and anger, then a story about who the enemy is. Asking for proof before the bombs fall is not betrayal. It is the lesson of Iraq.

When people chose people

The Christmas truce

Christmas 1914 · First World War

What happened
In parts of the front, British and German soldiers climbed out of their trenches. They swapped food and cigarettes, buried their dead together, sang carols and in places played football. Up to 100,000 may have taken part.
Why it matters
Generals ordered it not to happen again, and it never did on that scale. But for a few days, enemies chose to treat each other as people.

The Good Friday Agreement

10 April 1998 · Northern Ireland

What happened
A peace deal that largely ended the Troubles, a 30-year conflict often split along Catholic and Protestant lines, which killed more than 3,500 people.
Why it matters
Ordinary people voted for it in referendums on 22 May 1998: 71 percent in Northern Ireland and 94 percent in the Republic of Ireland.

The women of Liberia

2003 · Liberia and Ghana

What happened
Christian and Muslim women joined forces. They held protests and a sit-in, got the president to promise to join peace talks, then surrounded the negotiating room and kept up the pressure until the men made a deal.
Why it matters
Together with pressure from other countries, it helped end a 14-year civil war. Their leader, Leymah Gbowee, won the Nobel Peace Prize in 2011.

Common sense

“It says in the book”

  1. “It says in the book” is an argument that can be used for anything, so on its own it settles nothing. People have many different books, and each group believes its own is the true one. Nobody can prove their book is better than everyone else's. Most were written centuries or thousands of years ago, copied by hand, translated, lost in translation and read in many different ways. Words on a page do not make anyone entitled to kill.
  2. All people feel pain. All people love their children. Every person gets one life.
  3. Before people kill in large numbers, they often stop calling the other side people. They say enemy, infidel, savage, barbarian, terrorist, collateral, human animals. See the evidence below.
  4. So when you hear “it’s not murder, it’s justice” or “they’re not really people”, ask one question: would anyone accept the same thing if it were done to their own family?
  5. Many groups have called themselves chosen. Christians are “a chosen generation”. Muslims are “the best community ever raised for humanity”. Puritan settlers, and later presidents, called America “a city upon a hill”. If being chosen means anything good, it means more responsibility, not more worth.
