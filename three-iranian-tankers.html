THREE TANKERS

“Permanently disabled” is a staff-officer phrase. In simple words: a working ship was badly wrecked after the crew were told to get off.

What happened

Iranian reporting says the ships were hit, set on fire, and their crews were told to abandon them. Early reports said the three crews survived. That does not mean nothing happened. It only means no crew deaths were reported on these three ships at that time.

This war has already killed tanker sailors. Iranian officials also place the reported wedding strike in Sirik and the reported school strike in Minab in the same broader file of attacks.

Two timelines are still on the table

U.S. / CENTCOM account

Missiles directed at a U.S. carrier and a destroyer came first; the tanker strikes followed.

Iranian state-media sequence

Iranian media reported the Kharg tanker hit first, before reporting the later exchange.

Neither side has shown a clear, independent timeline proving exactly what happened first. Both sides are involved in the war, so treat both claims carefully.

Language matters

“Permanently disabled”
→ damaged so badly it could no longer work as a normal ship.
  • Use hit when that is what the source says.
  • Use set on fire when fire is documented.
  • Use abandon ship when crews were ordered off.
  • Do not turn “no reported casualties” into “nothing happened.”
  • Always say clearly who is making the claim.

Source standard
